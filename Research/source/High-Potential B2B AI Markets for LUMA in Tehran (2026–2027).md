# High-Potential B2B AI Markets for LUMA in Tehran, 2026–2027

## Research basis

**Direct answer:** LUMA’s strongest Tehran opportunity is not “AI for technology companies.” It is a **custom operational-automation layer for established mid-market distributors/importers and process-heavy manufacturers**—typically 50–500 employees, several sales/operations teams, a warehouse or factory, an accounting/ERP system, many PDFs/Excel files/messages, and no substantial internal AI team. The best opening wedge is **RFQ-to-quotation, order/document processing, inventory and purchasing analysis, management reporting, and internal knowledge search**, sold as a measurable process-improvement project rather than an AI subscription.

This conclusion is supported by the depth of Tehran’s industrial and trade base. Published figures cite 20 public industrial estates, 13 private estates and 38 industrial zones containing about 14,400 industrial units and more than 300,000 workers in Tehran Province; a June 2026 statement separately referred to more than 20,000 active production, service and workshop units in the 38 industrial zones. Shamsabad alone is reported at roughly 1,400 active industrial units, while Tehran Chamber states that it has more than 40,000 members. These are **ecosystem counts, not counts of qualified LUMA prospects**; the addressable market must be filtered for scale, activity, data readiness, management quality and internal technical capability.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

### Evidence labels

- **Verified:** directly supported by a cited public source.
- **Estimate:** a reasoned range derived from public footprints, directories or event/association coverage; not an official market count.
- **Planning assumption:** a commercial hypothesis for LUMA to test through interviews, pilots and win/loss data.
- Exact Tehran counts are unavailable for many categories. Exhibition participation is used only as evidence of sector depth and as a lead source—not as a proxy for the number of Tehran companies.

### Commercial value bands

No reliable public benchmark was found for Iranian custom-AI project pricing. The following are therefore **LUMA planning assumptions**, expressed in 2026 toman and intended to be re-priced quarterly:

| Band | First-year scope | Planning value |
|---|---|---:|
| A | Paid diagnostic or narrow proof of value | 0.6–1.5 billion toman |
| B | One departmental production workflow | 1.5–4 billion toman |
| C | Multi-workflow or multi-branch deployment | 4–12 billion toman |
| D | Enterprise, regulated or plant-wide program | 12+ billion toman |

Quarterly repricing is essential: World Bank open data reports 42.2% consumer-price inflation for Iran in 2025. Proposals should use milestone payments, short validity periods, a hardware/infrastructure pass-through clause, and an annual support amount indexed to labor and hosting costs.[^5]

## 1. Executive conclusion

### Best market

The priority market is **privately controlled, established B2B trading, distribution and manufacturing companies headquartered in Tehran, with operational sites across Tehran Province or nationwide**. The ideal firm already uses basic digital systems—accounting, ERP, CRM, warehouse or industry software—but employees still bridge systems with Excel, PDF, phone, email and messaging. Iran has mature local ERP, distribution and hospital-information-system products, so LUMA should not replace core systems; it should integrate them and automate the “last mile” of unstructured work.[^6][^7][^8]

The highest-conviction offer is a **Quote-to-Cash and Operations Copilot**:

1. Capture RFQs from email, website forms, PDFs, scanned letters and approved messaging channels.
2. Extract customer, SKU/specification, quantity, delivery and commercial terms.
3. Match the request to the product master, inventory, price lists and prior quotations.
4. Flag missing specifications, credit limits, low margin and approval requirements.
5. Draft a quotation and follow-up tasks for human approval.
6. Push approved records to CRM/ERP/accounting and expose pipeline, turnaround time and lost-RFQ reasons to management.

The financial case is concrete: **more RFQs processed per salesperson, lower quotation turnaround time, fewer pricing/specification errors, fewer lost leads and less administrative headcount growth**. For manufacturing, packaging and engineering firms, the same platform extends into bill-of-material/specification search, tender review, production documents, quality records and maintenance knowledge.

### Why this middle wins

- **Too digital:** major platforms, fintechs, telecoms and software companies often have engineering, data and product teams; external custom development competes with internal capacity.
- **Too small:** micro-retailers and independent professionals may have severe inefficiency but insufficient budget, data discipline or implementation capacity.
- **The profitable middle:** 50–500 employees; 10+ administrative, sales or operations users; multiple systems; recurring transaction/document volume; owner/CEO or COO accessibility; no dedicated AI team.

### Market-characteristic interpretation

- **Purchasing power:** strongest in import/distribution, manufacturing, pharma, medical equipment, EPC and multi-project construction.
- **Manual-work density:** strongest in RFQ-heavy distribution, packaging estimating, freight documents, tenders/contracts, field service and insurance paperwork.
- **Internal AI risk:** highest in digital platforms, large banks/telecoms, top retail/e-commerce, and the largest pharma/industrial groups.
- **Data readiness:** highest where an ERP/accounting/LIS/HIS/distribution system exists but does not handle unstructured documents or cross-department workflows.
- **Accessibility:** owner-managed mid-market firms, association members and exhibition participants are generally more reachable than state groups or public megacorporations.

## 3. Top priority segments

### Industrial distributors and importers

**Why first:** This segment has the cleanest combination of revenue, high-value transactions, repetitive RFQs, large catalogs, management access and moderate digital maturity. Many firms have accounting/ERP data but the commercial workflow still depends on salespeople interpreting PDFs, voice notes, email and calls.

- **Current process:** customer sends a list/specification; salesperson identifies SKUs, checks stock and supplier pricing, applies margin/credit rules, obtains approval, creates a PDF quotation and follows up manually.
- **Failure mode:** slow response, inconsistent pricing, missed alternatives, unlogged RFQs, duplicated entry and no visibility into reasons for lost quotes.
- **Build:** Persian/English RFQ ingestion, table/OCR extraction, product master matching, inventory and price lookup, approved substitutes, quote drafting, approval routing, CRM/ERP write-back and a turnaround/win-rate dashboard.
- **Users:** sales coordinators, product managers, commercial director, warehouse, finance and CEO.
- **Measured outcome:** median quotation time; RFQs per employee; quote error rate; gross-margin leakage; follow-up completion; quote-to-order conversion.
- **Required data:** 6–12 months of RFQs and quotes, product/SKU master, price lists, inventory feed, customer/credit data, approval rules and lost-order reasons.
- **Implementation:** highly realistic using local/on-prem infrastructure; start read-only and require human quote approval.

### Food/FMCG distribution

Iran Agrofood’s 2025 Tehran event included hundreds of domestic companies across food, food technology and agriculture, making exhibitor lists a high-yield source. The best targets are not the most advanced national giants, but regional/national firms with field sales, 2+ warehouses, many SKUs and visible hiring for sales operations, warehousing and reporting.[^24][^18]

- **Current process:** visit orders enter distribution software, while returns, out-of-stock substitutions, promotions, credit issues and branch summaries are handled by calls and spreadsheets.
- **Failure mode:** delayed exception response, expiry/slow-stock risk, inconsistent discounts, repeated manual reports and poor visibility by SKU/route/customer.
- **Build:** exception-centered operations layer—demand alerts, likely stockouts, return-reason classification, promotion/discount checks, voice/message-to-order capture and automated daily management narrative.
- **Users:** distribution manager, sales director, warehouse manager, finance and branch managers.
- **Measured outcome:** fill rate, returns/expiry value, order-entry time, stockout frequency, sales per visit, days inventory and hours spent on reporting.
- **Required data:** order lines, returns, inventory snapshots, routes/visits, customer hierarchy, promotions and receivables.
- **Implementation:** integrate rather than replace the distribution platform; Iranian products already digitize core visit/order/delivery processes.[^7][^25]

### Mid-market manufacturers

Tehran Province’s industrial-estate footprint provides a large funnel, but the target should be factories with meaningful administrative and production complexity, not every workshop. Good candidates show ISO certificates, multiple production lines, catalogs, QA/maintenance hiring, a dealer network or exports, but no AI/data-science hiring.[^3][^26]

- **Current process:** production planning uses ERP/accounting plus spreadsheets; work orders, QC sheets, shift reports, downtime notes and SOPs remain fragmented.
- **Failure mode:** planners manually consolidate data, defect knowledge is not searchable, repeated downtime is poorly coded, and management reports arrive late.
- **Build:** document/shift-report extraction, production exception dashboard, controlled SOP assistant, defect root-cause retrieval and maintenance troubleshooting copilot.
- **Users:** plant manager, production planning, QA/QC, maintenance, warehouse and COO.
- **Measured outcome:** report-preparation hours, schedule adherence, scrap/rework, unplanned downtime, mean time to repair and repeated defect rate.
- **Required data:** production orders, machine/line logs where available, QC records, downtime/maintenance history, BOM/routings and SOPs.
- **Implementation:** feasible with phased data cleanup; avoid autonomous process control and begin with decision support.

### Printing and packaging

The Tehran print/packaging exhibition reported 377 participants in its 2024 list and around 450 domestic/foreign participants in another account, confirming an active, discoverable ecosystem. Packaging firms are attractive because each custom job creates specification, estimating, artwork, approval and scheduling work.[^27][^15]

- **Current process:** sales receives dimensions/artwork; estimating calculates material, setup, waste, plates/dies, finishing and delivery; prepress manages revisions; production reschedules around machine constraints.
- **Failure mode:** incomplete briefs, quote delays, specification errors, version confusion, underestimated waste and repeated customer calls.
- **Build:** structured job-brief assistant, prior-job retrieval, rule-based/AI cost estimator, artwork/version checklist, approval portal and schedule-impact dashboard.
- **Users:** sales, estimator, prepress, production planning, procurement and general manager.
- **Measured outcome:** quote turnaround, estimator throughput, rework, material variance, on-time delivery and number of approval cycles.
- **Required data:** prior jobs and actual costs, machine rates/capacity, substrate/ink prices, waste rules, artwork versions and production outcomes.
- **Implementation:** high; keep final costing deterministic and explainable, using AI for extraction/matching rather than uncontrolled price generation.

### EPC and engineering contractors

The sector’s associations provide concentrated lead pools, including 215 installation/industrial-company members and a reported 285 oil/gas/petrochemical engineering and contracting members. Tehran headquarters frequently manage projects and document control even when sites are elsewhere.[^28][^23]

- **Current process:** bid teams read tender documents and BOQs; project teams exchange transmittals, RFIs, submittals, minutes, drawings, contracts and change correspondence.
- **Failure mode:** missed obligations, slow “where is the evidence?” search, duplicate review, untracked action items and weak claims/change-order documentation.
- **Build:** secure tender room, requirement/BOQ extractor, bid/no-bid checklist, clause/obligation register, correspondence search, meeting-action extraction and claims evidence timeline.
- **Users:** tender manager, contracts/commercial manager, document control, project controls, project manager and CEO.
- **Measured outcome:** bid-review hours, missed requirement count, RFI turnaround, overdue actions, recoverable change orders and time to assemble claim evidence.
- **Required data:** tender packages, contract templates, transmittal register, correspondence, schedules and cost codes.
- **Implementation:** technically realistic; security, permissions, versioning and audit trails are non-negotiable.

### Medical-equipment companies

Iran’s medical-equipment producer/exporter union is reported to have more than 130 member companies, and trade associations are headquartered in Tehran. Prioritize distributors with tenders, multiple product lines, hospital customers and a field-service installed base; avoid tiny stores and government-controlled procurement entities.[^29][^30]

- **Current process:** commercial staff match tender specifications to manufacturer catalogs, assemble regulatory/compliance documents, create quotes and coordinate installation/service.
- **Failure mode:** repeated spec comparison, expired/missing documents, slow response, inconsistent quote attachments and poor installed-base knowledge.
- **Build:** tender/specification matcher, controlled product dossier search, quotation pack generator, service-ticket triage, warranty/parts knowledge and renewal/calibration alerts.
- **Users:** commercial director, tender office, regulatory affairs, service manager and finance.
- **Measured outcome:** tenders screened per analyst, bid preparation time, missing-document rate, service first-time fix, contract renewal and quote win rate.
- **Required data:** product catalogs, registration/certification files, tender history, pricing, installed assets, service notes and parts inventory.
- **Implementation:** high if deployed locally with access controls; clinical recommendations should remain out of scope.

### Building materials and MEP suppliers

The 2026 Tehran construction exhibition reported 600–700 participants across materials, machinery, doors/windows, elevators, mechanical/electrical installations, safety and smart systems. Strong prospects are project-sales organizations, not commodity shops.[^20][^19]

- **Current process:** sales engineers receive BOQs/drawings/specifications, map them to products, request technical approvals, price the job and manage revisions.
- **Failure mode:** repeated manual matching, slow alternates, inconsistent technical submittals and poor follow-up across long project cycles.
- **Build:** BOQ/spec extractor, product matcher, compliant-alternative recommender, submittal pack generator, quotation version control and project pipeline dashboard.
- **Users:** project sales, technical office, commercial manager and CEO.
- **Measured outcome:** quote/submittal turnaround, lines matched per hour, revision errors, follow-up coverage and project conversion.
- **Required data:** catalog/spec sheets, certificates, price lists, inventory/lead times, previous BOQs/quotes and project CRM.
- **Implementation:** high; drawing interpretation can be phased after text/Excel/PDF BOQs.

### Logistics and freight forwarding

A public list of association members extends to at least 650 entries and contains many Tehran addresses, phones and emails; the association requires relevant transport licensing and chamber membership. This is a useful national lead pool that can be filtered to Tehran headquarters and mid-sized operators.[^31][^32]

- **Current process:** customers email booking instructions and shipment documents; operators re-key fields, chase missing documents, update status and prepare invoice/clearance packets.
- **Failure mode:** data-entry errors, shipment delays, repetitive customer updates and no unified exception queue.
- **Build:** invoice/packing-list/BOL extraction, document completeness checker, shipment exception workflow, customer status assistant and profitability dashboard.
- **Users:** operations manager, documentation team, customer service, finance and managing director.
- **Measured outcome:** minutes per file, data corrections, missing-document delays, shipments per operator, status calls and gross profit per shipment.
- **Required data:** past shipment files, standard forms, TMS/Excel exports, email, customer master and status milestones.
- **Implementation:** local document processing is feasible; external customs/carrier integrations may be unstable, so email/file-based fallback is needed.

## 4. Segments to avoid

| Segment | Why it looks attractive | Why it is usually poor | Exception |
|---|---|---|---|
| Major fintechs, e-commerce platforms, ride-hailing and software firms | Large data and visible AI use | Strong engineering/product/data teams; high internal-build preference; supplier security review | A specialized capability they cannot staff, with a technical buyer and clear API/infrastructure scope |
| Large banks, telecoms and top insurers | Huge document and customer volume | Long procurement, security and regulatory barriers; incumbents and internal teams; payment risk | Subcontract through an approved integrator or a narrow non-core pilot |
| State ministries, municipalities and state megagroups | Large process inefficiency | Tendering, political/procurement complexity, long receivables and security constraints | Funded private subsidiary with autonomous procurement |
| Micro-wholesalers, single-location shops and independent professionals | Obvious manual work | Low budget, low data quality, owner overload and insufficient recurring volume | Productized template with minimal customization, not LUMA’s core custom model |
| Tiny clinics and single physician practices | Calls, documents and scheduling | Small budget and sensitive data; numerous low-value custom requests | Multi-branch group or centralized call center |
| Early-stage startups | AI-friendly management | Cash constraints, changing process and internal developers | Venture-funded scale-up without an AI team and a short paid engagement |
| Top-tier digital retailers/chains | Rich POS/customer data | Strong internal IT/BI, integration politics and long vendor process | Business-owned workflow outside the core roadmap |
| Pure social-media sellers and online-only microbusinesses | High automation need | Fragile economics and connectivity exposure | Aggregator, franchise or service provider selling to thousands of them |
| Distressed or stalled construction developers | Document and project complexity | Weak cash flow, delayed projects and collection risk | Visible active projects, presales, hiring and current supplier activity |
| Autonomous clinical diagnosis or credit/claims decisioning | High theoretical value | High liability, explainability and privacy burden | Human-assistive triage with explicit clinical/compliance ownership |
| Generic content-generation projects | Easy to demonstrate | Commoditized, hard to defend, weak measurable ROI | Connected content operations tied to catalog data, approvals and conversion |

The current operating environment increases the value of local-first software but weakens internet-dependent microbusinesses. Iranian reporting in 2026 described roughly 120 days of disruption or outages during the preceding Iranian year, while broader reporting documented severe effects on trade and digital businesses. LUMA should therefore avoid architectures that require continuous access to foreign APIs and should not make connectivity-fragile businesses its primary customer base.[^33][^34][^35]

### ROI standard

Every proposal should calculate:

`Annual net benefit = labor capacity released + errors/losses avoided + incremental gross profit + working-capital benefit − annual operating cost`

`Payback months = project cost ÷ average monthly net benefit`

Use the customer’s own fully loaded labor cost, transaction volume, baseline error/loss rate, gross margin and inventory/receivable cost. Do not promise headcount reduction when the more credible benefit is **volume growth without proportional hiring**. A pilot should establish baseline and post-pilot measurements before a full rollout.

### Iran implementation constraints

- **Local-first:** deploy core workflows, model serving, vector search, logs and backups in Iran or on customer premises; support queued operation during international outages. Recent disruption reporting makes foreign-API-only architecture commercially unsafe.[^36][^34]
- **Cloud/sanctions:** OFAC rules authorize some fee-based cloud services connected to internet communications, but this is not blanket permission for every commercial enterprise-AI workload; service-provider terms and sanctions screening still matter. Use Iranian infrastructure or self-hosted models by default and obtain specialist legal advice for foreign services.[^37][^38][^39]
- **Privacy:** Articles 58–59 of Iran’s Electronic Commerce Law require explicit consent for sensitive personal data and specify purpose limitation, proportional collection, accuracy, access/correction and deletion concepts. Health, HR, voice and customer data therefore require minimization, access controls, retention rules and audit logs.[^40][^41][^42]
- **Legacy integration:** expect CSV/Excel, database views, print templates and robotic/browser automation where APIs are absent. Begin read-only; add write-back only after reconciliation tests.
- **Persian documents:** benchmark OCR on the customer’s actual scans, tables, stamps, handwriting and mixed Persian/English numerals. Route low-confidence fields to human review.
- **Cybersecurity:** isolate tenants, encrypt data at rest/in transit, use role-based access, redact secrets, maintain prompt/data logs and define incident response. Financial organizations have been reported as a growing cyberattack target in Iran.[^43]

## 8. Company scoring system

### Base formula

Score every company on normalized dimensions from 0 to 1. Let `d` be digital maturity from 0 to 100 and define the digital sweet-spot function:

`G(d) = exp(-((d - 55) / 24)^2)`

This peaks near **moderate maturity (55/100)** and falls at both extremes. The raw prospect score is:

`Raw = 12C + 12O + 10R + 16V + 10D + 12G(d) + 8A + 6E + 5P + 4F + 3S + 2L`

| Symbol | Weight | Meaning | High-score evidence |
|---|---:|---|---|
| C | 12 | Company strength/purchasing power | Multiple sites, national clients, active hiring, facilities, credible brands, stable operations |
| O | 12 | Operational complexity | Multiple departments/branches, warehouse/plant/projects, approvals and handoffs |
| R | 10 | Repetitive workflow intensity | High RFQ/order/document/ticket/report volume |
| V | 16 | Quantifiable AI value | Labor, margin, conversion, downtime, inventory or error KPI with sizable baseline |
| D | 10 | Usable digital information | ERP/CRM/WMS/LIS exports, PDFs, email, historical transactions, product master |
| G(d) | 12 | Moderate-maturity sweet spot | Some systems/data but visible fragmentation; score declines if almost paper-only or highly advanced |
| A | 8 | Internal capability gap | No AI/data team; small IT function; relies on vendors |
| E | 6 | Decision-maker reachability | Owner/CEO visibility, association/event access, direct contacts, warm paths |
| P | 5 | Expected project size | Departmental or cross-functional value, 10+ users, recurring volume |
| F | 4 | Implementation feasibility | Clear owner, available samples, manageable integrations, human approval possible |
| S | 3 | Sales-cycle ease | Private procurement, few approval layers, paid-pilot acceptance |
| L | 2 | Regulatory ease | Low-risk data and decision support rather than regulated autonomous decisions |

Here `A` is **high when internal AI capability is low**. A firm with a visible ML/data-science group should receive a low `A`, even if every other dimension looks attractive.

### Adjustments and gates

`Final score = clamp(Raw + trigger bonuses − risk penalties, 0, 100)`

**Trigger bonuses, maximum +5:** active ERP/CRM change (+2), branch/plant expansion (+1), high-volume hiring in the affected function (+1), new product/dealership/tender (+1), public service/backlog issue (+1).

**Risk penalties, maximum −25:** unpaid/prolonged procurement (−3 to −8), financial distress/inactivity (−5 to −15), government/sanctions exposure (−3 to −10), severe privacy/clinical scope (−3 to −8), no usable data (−5 to −12), no executive sponsor (−5), foreign-API-only requirement (−3).

**Hard disqualifiers:** inactive/dissolved entity; no credible operating footprint; expected first-year value below Band A; request to make unreviewed clinical/legal/credit decisions; refusal to provide process owner, sample data or security requirements; clearly strong internal AI team unless partnership demand is explicit.

### Interpretation

| Score | Action |
|---:|---|
| 80–100 | Tier 1: immediate human research and tailored outreach |
| 70–79 | Tier 2: qualify with one more evidence source or trigger |
| 60–69 | Nurture/experiment; use productized diagnostic |
| 45–59 | Low priority; outreach only with warm referral or strong trigger |
| Below 45 | Exclude |

### Scoring discipline

Store the score for every component, its evidence URL, date and confidence. Do not let missing data default to a positive score; use neutral values and lower confidence. Re-score after discovery calls, because public signals can reveal scale and maturity but cannot prove transaction volume, data quality, authority or budget.

|---|---|---:|

### Strength and distress signals

**Strength:** current certifications, recent exhibitions, active jobs, new branches/lines, current news, multiple named executives, national customers, export activity, large facilities and consistent contact information.

**Distress/inactivity:** dissolved/inactive registry status, expired domain, unreachable phones across sources, last update several years old, persistent layoffs/closure reports, stalled projects, no recent hiring or activity, repeated address conflicts and unusually aggressive advance-payment complaints. Treat reviews and social comments as leads, not verified facts.

### Evidence standard for proposal generation

An automated agent should not write a personalized proposal until it has at least:

1. **Identity:** official name, registry status/ID, website and Tehran location.
2. **Scale:** two independent signals such as employees, branches, facility, clients, hiring or event participation.
3. **Business model:** products/services, customer type, sales channels and operational footprint.
4. **Workflow evidence:** at least two concrete clues—RFQ language, catalog/BOQ, tender pages, forms, service network, job roles, distribution/dealer network, support instructions or downloadable documents.
5. **Digital baseline:** visible portal/forms/software hiring/technology references plus gaps.
6. **Internal tech assessment:** technical job count, department references and AI/data signals.
7. **Decision-maker evidence:** named executive/functional leader from website, official gazette, association, LinkedIn or news.
8. **Freshness:** one source updated within 12 months where possible.

Every generated finding must be labeled **Observed**, **Strong inference**, **Weak inference** or **Unknown**. The proposal should cite observed facts, phrase inferences as hypotheses (“This may be creating…”) and ask a diagnostic question rather than pretending to know the internal process.

- `company_id`, `proposal_version`, `target_contact_id`
- `personalized_observation`, `hypothesized_pain`, `proposed_wedge`
- `business_case_inputs`, `estimated_value_range`, `assumptions`

- `human_approved`, `approval_notes`, `sent_at`, `channel`, `campaign_id`

### Data governance

- Preserve raw Persian text and normalized fields; never overwrite source spelling.
- Store every score with source-level evidence, date and confidence.
- Separate **not found** from **does not exist**.
- Track robots/terms compliance, crawl rate, public-data purpose and removal requests.
- Do not collect private mobile numbers, personal emails or sensitive personal data without a lawful and justified purpose. Iran’s e-commerce law treats private-personal and health data as sensitive and requires explicit consent for processing in relevant contexts.[^41][^40]
- Re-crawl high-priority records every 90 days, medium priority every 180 days and legal status annually or before outreach.

## 12. Recommended acquisition strategy

### Stage 1 — Validate the thesis

Conduct 25–40 structured interviews across the top eight segments before scaling the database. Use association introductions, exhibition exhibitors, existing networks and industrial-estate visits. Capture transaction volume, current tools, cycle time, error/loss cost, process owner, data availability, integration constraints, buying authority and willingness to pay for a paid diagnostic.

**Exit criterion:** at least three segments with repeated pain, 5+ qualified firms each, an identifiable KPI and a feasible first workflow. Do not build a generic platform first.

### Stage 2 — Build the discovery graph

1. Ingest official industrial-unit lists, association/member lists and current exhibition catalogs.
2. Add Book-e Avval, Google Maps, Neshan, Balad, job platforms and targeted search results.
3. Resolve legal entities through national-ID/official-gazette sources.
4. Normalize names, Persian characters, phones, domains, addresses and coordinates.
5. Deduplicate by national ID, domain, phone, address similarity and alias graph.

Start with 5,000–10,000 raw records across the top eight categories, then expect aggressive filtering. A raw listing is not a prospect.

### Stage 3 — Automated qualification

Run lightweight classification before expensive crawling:

- Exclude inactive entities, microbusinesses, software/AI vendors and obvious government entities.
- Compute company-strength and scale proxies.
- Detect catalog, branches, factory/warehouse, clients/projects, certificates, service network and job activity.
- Detect internal tech capability and apply the capability penalty.
- Apply the digital sweet-spot function, not a monotonic maturity score.
- Promote only records with sufficient evidence and confidence.

**Output:** Tier 1/2/3 queues plus explicit exclusion reasons. Sample and manually audit at least 10% of each tier until precision stabilizes.

### Stage 4 — Deep website analysis

For Tier 1–2 records, crawl pages relevant to operations: products, catalogs, quote/order forms, tenders, projects, clients, branches/dealers, after-sales, downloads, certifications, jobs, contact, FAQ and news. Extract observed facts and map them to a sector process library. The agent must show the evidence beside every inference.

A credible opportunity statement follows this pattern:

> **Observed:** the company publishes 14 product-category PDFs, offers project quotations and lists three service centers. **Inference:** sales/service staff may manually search specifications and assemble quote/service information. **Diagnostic question:** how many monthly inquiries require a specialist to identify products or past service history? **Potential wedge:** a permissioned catalog-and-service assistant connected to inventory and ticket history.

### Stage 5 — Opportunity detection

Generate no more than three hypotheses per company, ranked by:

1. Evidence strength.
2. KPI size and executive relevance.
3. Data availability.
4. Implementation feasibility within 8–12 weeks.
5. Ability to expand after a successful pilot.

Prefer a narrow transactional wedge over a generic chatbot. Examples: RFQ-to-quote for a distributor, tender screening for an EPC, estimate-to-job for packaging, service triage for equipment, or returns/stock exceptions for distribution.

### Stage 6 — Personalized proposal

Each proposal should contain:

- Two verified company-specific observations.
- One explicitly labeled workflow hypothesis.
- A diagnostic question that tests volume and pain.
- A one-workflow solution with human approval and named integrations.
- Three baseline KPIs and a proposed proof-of-value method.
- Local/on-prem deployment and security position.
- A low-friction call to action: 45-minute process mapping, followed by a paid diagnostic if qualified.

Do not claim a fixed ROI from public data. Present a calculator using customer inputs and a target payback range to be validated.

### Stage 7 — Human review

Before sending, a Persian-speaking analyst should verify company identity, current activity, contact role, evidence quality, sensitivity, technical feasibility and tone. Reject messages that merely say “AI can improve efficiency,” infer confidential operations as fact, or contain unsupported employee/revenue estimates.

### Stage 8 — Outreach

Use an account-based sequence:

1. Warm association/exhibitor/customer introduction where available.
2. Short Persian email or LinkedIn message to the operational owner.
3. Telephone follow-up referencing the business process, not “AI transformation.”
4. A one-page process hypothesis and KPI worksheet.
5. Invite 3–5 similar non-competing companies to a sector-specific workshop.

LUMA should maintain sector-specific proof assets: anonymized before/after metrics, process maps, sample documents, architecture/security sheet and a live narrow demo. Case studies should be organized by workflow and KPI, not by model or technology.

### Stage 9 — Paid diagnostic and pilot

**Diagnostic, 1–2 weeks:** process map, baseline, data sample, risk assessment, integration design, ROI model and fixed-scope pilot proposal.

**Pilot, 6–10 weeks:** one department, one workflow, historical test set, human approval, audit logs and weekly KPI review. A pilot proceeds to production only if acceptance thresholds are met for extraction accuracy, turnaround, adoption, error reduction and economic value.

### Stage 10 — Production and expansion

Harden permissions, monitoring, backups, fallback operation, model/version controls and support SLAs. Expand adjacent workflows only after the first KPI is sustained: RFQ → order → purchasing → inventory; tender → contract → project correspondence; service intake → dispatch → parts → renewal.

### First 180 days

| Period | Action | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1–30 | Interview 30 companies; select three wedges; create KPI calculators | Validated pain library and offer definitions |
| Days 31–60 | Build 2,000–3,000 high-quality records from associations, exhibitions and industrial lists | Deduplicated master table with evidence |
| Days 61–90 | Score, audit and deeply research top 300 accounts | 100–150 Tier 1 accounts and contacts |
| Days 91–120 | Send manually reviewed, segment-specific outreach; run workshops | 20–30 discovery calls; 6–10 diagnostic candidates |
| Days 121–180 | Deliver 3–5 paid diagnostics and 2–3 pilots | First quantified case studies and refined scoring weights |

### Sales dashboard

Track qualified-account rate, evidence completeness, contact accuracy, positive reply rate, meeting rate, diagnostic conversion, paid-pilot conversion, pilot-to-production conversion, median sales cycle, gross margin, collection days, implementation hours and realized customer KPI. Feed win/loss and implementation data back into the scoring weights every quarter.

## Final commercial recommendation

LUMA should position itself as a **local operational-AI engineering partner** for companies that are already large enough to feel process pain but not sophisticated enough to solve it internally. The first campaign should focus on **industrial distributors/importers, food/FMCG distribution, mid-market manufacturers, printing/packaging, EPC/engineering, medical equipment, building-material/MEP suppliers and freight forwarders**.

The wedge should be a paid process diagnostic leading to one of four repeatable solution families: **RFQ/order automation, document/tender intelligence, operations/management reporting, or service/knowledge automation**. Commercial proof must always follow the chain:

`Observed process evidence → validated bottleneck → scoped integration → human-reviewed AI workflow → baseline KPI → measured financial result`

That sequence protects LUMA from the digital-maturity paradox: it avoids both companies able to build internally and businesses too small to fund change, while concentrating on Tehran’s established, operationally complex and under-integrated middle market.
